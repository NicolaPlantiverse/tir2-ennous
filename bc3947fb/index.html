The problem

AI is arriving in every portfolio.
Few can govern it.

Adopting a model is easy; governing it is hard. Before an LP, a regulator or a field trusts a number, a handful of questions decide everything, and most organisations, however expert in their own domain, cannot answer one of them.

Q1

Which model, and why this one?

Is it the best model for this decision, or simply the one already installed? Every question deserves the analysis that fits it, not a default.
Q2

When is it even worth it?

Not every decision needs a heavy model. When does AI add value over its cost and risk, and when is the simplest method the honest one?
Q3

All the signal, or the convenient part?

Is every useful source captured and brought to one comparable grammar, or only the data that was easy to reach?
Q4

Out of the black box?

Can the reasoning be opened and read, step by step, or is trusting the output an act of faith?
Q5

Efficiency and error, measured?

How much compute, and how much uncertainty around the answer, quantified and tracked, not assumed?
Q6

Who governs the models, and what emerges?

When many models run, what governs them? And what pattern surfaces across them that no single model, and no human query, could reach alone?

Ennous is the brain that governs the brains, it chooses among models, gathers the signal, opens the reasoning, quantifies efficiency and error, and leaves a proof. By construction. Then it applies that where measurement is hardest: the working forest.
